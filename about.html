About me

I am a PhD candidate in Finance at Aalto University School of Business in Finland.

My research lies at the intersection of behavioral finance, household finance, and experimental economics. I am particularly interested in how investors' personal experiences are encoded, remembered, and used when they make later financial decisions.

I use both large-scale investor transaction data and controlled experiments. This combination allows me to study behavior in real financial markets while using experiments to isolate mechanisms that are difficult to identify from observational data alone.

Research interests

My current interests include investor memory and experience, selling and repurchasing behavior, associative representations of portfolios, return frequency, investment horizons, and the effects of information presentation on financial choice.

Methods

My empirical work includes survival analysis, panel-data methods, and the construction and analysis of high-frequency investor histories. For experimental projects, I design and deploy online and laboratory studies. I primarily work with Stata, Python, MATLAB, SQL, and oTree.
